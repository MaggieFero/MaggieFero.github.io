CFPs can be intimidating, whether they Call For Papers, Presentations, Posters, or just Proposals more generally. There's a vulnerability to sharing your ideas for selection. Personally, my CFP experience has gotten much better since I started thinking of the talks I propose as "offerings," rather than "proposals" or "submissions".

If I offer you a pen and you decline, I don't think "this person hates me" or "nobody likes pens" or any of those anxious thoughts that sometimes come with a CFP rejection. I probably don't think about it a second time at all unless it's a REALLY weird refusal in some way.

I know as a frequent reviewer that the valence of a CFP rejection is pretty similar to declining a pen from the committee side. It _might_ be a bad proposal, but much more often it's just not what we need for our community right now. Submissions are typically rejected because others are similar, others are too different for it to add to a cohesive narrative, or we just plain don't have enough space to take all the great submissions we've received.

Reframing declined submissions as "I offered something they don't need" lets me, as a submitter, get past the thought that maybe I was the ONE bad submission that always turns up in a CFP. (If you think this is you: you've thought more about your proposal than the person who submitted that one, every time.)

Thinking of previously-declined submissions as offers that didn't meet a need for that particular conference at this particular time also encourages re-submitting an updated version the next time a CFP opens! This lets my talks find their most appropriate home and evolve with the field over time.
